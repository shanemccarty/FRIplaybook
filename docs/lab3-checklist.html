19  Lab 3 Checklist

What you should have when Map Your Analysis is done

Author

Shane McCarty

Published

10.05.2026

Abstract

This short chapter closes Lab 3. It first sums up each Map Your Analysis chapter in three parts: what you learned, what you did in the lab (and whether it involved any code in RStudio), and what you will do in Your Turn with your own research questions. It then lists what should exist in your head, in your lab file, and in your report draft, with a one-line way to check each item and a link back to the section that made it. Lab 3 has little code. Its product is a decision: for each of your research questions, which box on the Analysis Map it lands in and which line of that box, written down as the Data Analysis Plan paragraph of your Methods section.

Keywords

checklist, Lab 3, analysis plan

Lab 3 is different from Labs 1 and 2. There is almost no code to run, and nothing new goes into data/. What you make is a decision for each research question, and the test of whether you made it is whether you can write it down. So this checklist has fewer rows, and most of them are things you should be able to say or write rather than things that should be on your disk. (If you are not sure what a Lab checklist is for, Lab 1 Checklist explains the two kinds.)

19.1 What each chapter asked of you

The three parts are the same as in Lab 2: what you learned by reading, what you did yourself in the lab, and what you will do in Your Turn for your report. The Where column says whether the lab part involved code:

  • No code: you answered on paper or in your head and checked yourself against Ref.
  • RStudio, lab data: you ran code in Lastname_lab3.qmd on the lab dataset.
  • Read: there was nothing to run.

GoGo: this is where the two kinds of checklist meet

The Data Analysis Plan is the one Lab item that goes straight into your report. From here on, every chapter you need is chosen by that plan: the box your question lands in tells you which chapter in Lab 4 or Lab 5 to read, and that chapter’s Your Turn checklist tells you what your report must contain. Students on the mixed-methods track need one box; students on the quantitative track need two, usually one from each side of the map.

Caution Ref’s call: the gaps that send you to the wrong chapter
  • Your plan says “compare” but your two variables are both scores → that is a relationship; you are on the right side of the map. Cutting a score into high and low to make it a comparison is the foul Relate 3+ Variables warns about.
  • Your plan says “pre/post” but different people took the two surveys → that is two groups, not one group twice: Compare 2 Groups, not Compare 1 Group, Pre/Post.
  • Your outcome is a single item and your plan picked the parametric line → go back to the Likert Go box. The Mann-Whitney U, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Kruskal-Wallis, and Spearman chapters are written for exactly this case.
  • Your plan is in the future tense → it will read as a promise in the Final Report. Write what you did, and bracket what you have not done yet.

19.3 What Labs 4 and 5 will add

From here the playbook branches. Lab 4 (Compare Groups) and Lab 5 (Examine Relationships) each have a visualization chapter and then one chapter per box on the map. Read the ones your Data Analysis Plan points to; each has its own Ref’s checks on the lab dataset and its own Your Turn checklist for your report, so there is no separate Lab checklist for them.
