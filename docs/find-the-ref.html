6  Find the Ref

How to start and end every coding session

Author

Shane McCarty, PhD

Published

09.20.2026

Abstract

This chapter introduces Ref the Raccoon, the referee of this playbook, and the routine he runs at the start and the end of every coding session. Researchers learn the six steps to reopen their RStudio Project and get their data, packages, and variables back, the four steps to finish a work session and back up their files to their ELN, how to fix the problems that most often appear at the start of a session, and where Ref shows up in the rest of the playbook (The Play, The Lab, and Your Turn). Use this chapter at the start of every lab and every time you sit down to work on your report.

Keywords

Ref the Raccoon, RStudio project, routine, load library, environment, backup, ELN, troubleshooting

Meet Ref. I am Ref the Raccoon. A referee is there to keep the game fair and to help you play it right. Find me first, every time you sit down to code, and find me last, before you leave. This chapter is my routine. It takes about a minute at the start and a minute at the end, and it prevents most of the problems that stop people in their tracks.

6.1 Why you need this chapter: the kitchen

Think of RStudio as a kitchen.

In the kitchen In RStudio
The recipe Your code, saved in your .qmd file
The groceries in the pantry Your data files, saved in your project folder
The countertop while you cook R’s memory (the Environment tab): loaded packages, imported data, new variables

When you close RStudio, the countertop is wiped clean. The recipe and the groceries are still there. Your files are safe. The countertop is not. That is on purpose, for two reasons.

1. Leftovers ruin the dish. While you cook, the countertop fills up: a sauce you made before you fixed the recipe, or an ingredient from a step you later crossed out. If you keep cooking with what is on the counter, the dish can taste fine and still not match the recipe. In R, a plot or a statistic can look fine and still not match the code in your file, and R will not warn you. When you start with a clean counter and cook from the top of the recipe, every result comes from the code that is actually written down.

2. Someone else has to cook it, too. Your teammate and your instructor do not have your countertop. They only have your recipe and your groceries (your .qmd file and your data file). If they follow the recipe in their own kitchen, they should get the same dish: the same plots and the same statistics. That is what reproducible means. A recipe that only works with yesterday’s leftovers is not a recipe, and next month you will not be able to cook it either.

ImportantImportant: Clean counter, cook from the top

Every time you sit down to work, start with an empty Environment and re-run your code from the top. It takes about a minute, and it proves that your work is reproducible.

6.1.1 How to clean the counter

Objects from earlier work (alldata, select_data, VARIABLE1) stay in the environment tab until you clear them. If you turned off “Restore .RData” in R Software, the counter is already clean every time you open RStudio. During a session, there are two ways to wipe it:

  • Session > Restart R. This is the cleanest option: it empties the environment and unloads every package, so what happens next is exactly what a render will see.
  • rm(list = ls()) in the console. This removes every object but leaves packages loaded. Use it when you want a quick reset without restarting.
# Remove all objects from the global environment
rm(list = ls())

Then run your load-library chunk and continue from the top.

6.2 Once per computer

You did most of this when you first set up your computer. You do not repeat it each time.

  • Install R and RStudio (R Software)
  • Create your RStudio Project, Lastname_FRI
  • Run your install.R file to install your packages (R Packages)
  • Create your first report file, with its load-library chunk (Start a Report). You repeat this once per lab, not per session.
  • Create a folder called R Labs inside your individual ELN (your Google Drive folder). In Google Drive, open your ELN and click + New > New folder.

6.3 Ref’s kickoff: every time you start RStudio

  1. Open your project, not a file. Find your Lastname_FRI folder and double-click Lastname_FRI.Rproj. (Or, in RStudio: File > Open Project…)
  2. Check that you are in your project. The project name Lastname_FRI appears in the top right corner of RStudio, and the files tab shows your data file and your .qmd files.
  3. Open the .qmd file you are working on by clicking it in the files tab.
  4. Run your load-library chunk. It is the first code chunk in your .qmd. Click the green ▶ arrow in the top right corner of the chunk.
  5. Run everything above where you stopped. Click inside the chunk where you want to start working. Then click the Run All Chunks Above button (the gray ▼ with a line under it, next to the green arrow). Your data and variables are now back in the environment tab.
  6. Keep going. Start writing and running code where you left off.
GoGo: Starting a new lab? Make a new file first

The six steps above are for a day when you continue a file you already started. On the first day of a new lab there is no file to open yet. Do steps 1 and 2, then create and name the new .qmd file and paste in your load-library chunk exactly as in Start a Report (the previous chapter). Save it, check that it appears in the files tab, and pick up at step 4.

6.4 Ref’s final whistle: before you leave

Keep counting. Steps 7 to 10 finish your session and back up your work.

  1. Save your .qmd file (Cmd + S on Mac / Ctrl + S on Windows). If the file name at the top of the script editor is red with a *, it is not saved.
  2. Render your .qmd file. To render means to turn your .qmd file into a finished report. Click the Render button, the blue arrow at the top of the script editor. R then starts with an empty memory, runs all of your code from the first line to the last, and builds a report (a web page) with your writing, your plots, and your results. It can take a minute, and the report opens when it is done.
    • If the report appears, your code works from top to bottom. You will be able to pick up next time without surprises.
    • If it stops with an error, that is useful, too: it shows you the first line that does not work. If you cannot fix it today, type a note to yourself at that spot, starting with # (for example, # FIX: error on this line, object not found), so you know where to start next time.
  3. Back up your files to your ELN. Open your individual ELN (your Google Drive folder) in your browser and open your R Labs folder. Click + New > File upload, go to your Lastname_FRI project folder, and select the files you worked on today: your .qmd files and your .R files (such as install.R). You can also drag the files from your project folder into the browser window. If Google Drive asks what to do with a file that is already there, choose Replace existing file. Google Drive keeps the older versions for you.
  4. Quit RStudio. If RStudio asks to save your workspace, choose Don’t Save, because the workspace is the countertop, not the recipe. It holds the leftovers from today: your datasets and variables, including any made by code you later changed or deleted. If you save it, R puts those leftovers back the next time you open RStudio, and your results may no longer match your code. Your .qmd file and your data file are already saved, and they are all you need to make everything again.
CautionCaution: Save your file before you close RStudio

RStudio does not save your .qmd or .R file for you. If the file name on its tab is red with a *, your latest changes exist only on the screen, and closing RStudio throws them away. Two different questions come up when you quit, and they need opposite answers.

Do this

  • Press Cmd + S (Mac) or Ctrl + S (Windows) every few minutes, and always before you Render. The file name on the tab turns black when it is saved.
  • Before you quit, click File > Save All.
  • If RStudio asks “Save changes to report.qmd?”, click Save. That question is about your file.
  • Copy your .qmd and .R files to your ELN at the end of every session (Find the Ref).

Do not do this

  • Do not close RStudio, or your laptop, while a file name is still red.
  • Do not click Don’t Save when the question names a file (report.qmd, install.R). Only the question about the workspace (.RData) gets Don’t Save: the workspace is leftovers, and your file is the recipe.
  • Do not rely on Render to save for you. It usually does, but only for the file you rendered, and only if the render starts.
ImportantImportant: Close the project, reopen it, and run everything before you submit

Code that worked while you were editing can still fail from a clean start, because something you ran earlier by hand (a package, an object, a data file in the wrong place) was propping it up. Before you submit your RD Report or Final Report, and before you publish it, prove that it runs on its own:

  1. Close the project: File > Close Project. If RStudio asks about the workspace, choose Don’t Save.
  2. Open the project fresh: double-click Lastname_FRI.Rproj. The environment tab should be empty.
  3. Run all of the code from the top. For a .qmd report, click Render; it starts a fresh R session and runs every chunk from the first line to the last, the same job Source does for an .R script.
  4. Check the result. The report should open with every plot, table, and number in place, and the console should show no errors. If it stops, fix the first error, then start again from step 1.

Only when a fresh run works from top to bottom is the report ready to submit and publish. If it works on your computer this way, it will work on your instructor’s.

ImportantImportant: Your ELN is your backup

Laptops get lost, broken, and replaced. Your individual ELN is a Google Drive folder that does not. Get in the habit of backing up to your R Labs folder at the end of every work session, not only when something is due. A backup takes less than a minute. Redoing a lab or a report takes hours.

6.5 If something goes wrong when you come back

What you see What it means The fix
could not find function "read_excel" (or another function) The package is not loaded in this session. Run your load-library chunk (step 4).
object 'mydata' not found R forgot the object when you closed RStudio. Run All Chunks Above (step 5).
cannot open file ... No such file or directory or path does not exist R is looking in the wrong folder, or the file is not in your project. Check step 2. If the project name is missing in the top right corner, you opened a file and not the project. Go back to step 1.
there is no package called ‘psych’ The package is not installed on this computer. Add the package to your install.R file and run install.R again. Then run your load-library chunk.
The files tab does not show your files You are not in your project. File > Open Project… and choose Lastname_FRI.Rproj.
Your laptop is lost or broken, or a file is gone Your project folder is gone, but your backup is not. Download your files from the R Labs folder in your ELN into a new Lastname_FRI project.

Still stuck? Restart R (Session > Restart R) and start again at step 4. A fresh start fixes more problems than you would expect.

6.6 Ref’s Quick Checklist

The whole routine on one line each. Every coding chapter from here on opens and closes with a gray Ref box that links back to this list.

Step Do
🏁 Kickoff Open Project (Lastname_FRI.Rproj) → Open .qmd → Run load-library chunk → Run All Chunks Above → Code
🔔 Final whistle Save (Cmd/Ctrl + S) → Render → Back up to ELN (R Labs folder) → Quit, Don’t Save workspace
🚑 Something wrong? Session > Restart R, then start at the kickoff. Still stuck: the table above.

6.7 Where you will find Ref in this playbook

Most chapters that use data have the same three sections, and you will go through them with three different datasets.

Section Dataset What you do
The Play an example dataset Watch the play. Read the code and the output, step by step.
The Lab the lab dataset Run the play. Try it yourself during your R Lab, then check your own work. Labs are not graded.
Your Turn your team’s dataset Call your own play. After you finish all of the R Labs, come back and run the play for your own research questions.

Ref does not talk all the time. Look for him in these places:

  • Ref’s kickoff opens every coding chapter and The Lab, and Ref’s final whistle closes them. They are one-line reminders that link to the Quick Checklist above.
  • Ref’s check boxes in The Lab show you the answer you should get, so you can check your own work. Try the play first, then open the box.
  • A foul is a mistake that gives you a wrong answer without any error message. These are the mistakes you cannot catch on your own, so Ref blows the whistle.
  • In Your Turn, there is no Ref. It’s game time! You run the play with your own data and check your own work with the same checklist your instructor uses.
